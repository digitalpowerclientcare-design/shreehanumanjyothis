## The seven questions

**1. Do you guarantee results?**
The answer should be no. Charts indicate tendencies and timing. They do not determine whether a marriage happens, a job comes through, or a person returns. Anyone answering yes is either misunderstanding the method or misrepresenting it.

**2. What details do you need, and what if my birth time is uncertain?**
A genuine practitioner asks for date, exact time and place, and tells you honestly what an uncertain birth time does to the reliability of the reading. Someone who says the time does not matter for a full chart reading is not reading the chart properly.

**3. Do you sell the remedies you recommend?**
Not disqualifying, but worth knowing. When the same person recommends and sells the gemstone, the recommendation is harder to evaluate.

**4. Can you explain why this remedy, for this placement?**
A remedy should come with reasoning you can follow. "Your chart requires it" is not reasoning. If the explanation cannot be given in plain terms, that is informative.

**5. What will you not be able to tell me?**
Every method has limits. A practitioner who can describe theirs is more likely to be working within them. One who implies the chart can answer anything is not.

**6. Is there anything here that needs a doctor, a lawyer or a counsellor?**
For health, legal and serious relational matters, the right answer is sometimes yes. Someone willing to say so is someone worth consulting.

**7. What does this cost, and is anything added later?**
The fee should be stated before you commit, in full, including any site visit or materials.

## Claims that should end the conversation

- **Guaranteed marriage, job, reconciliation or business outcome.** None of it is supportable.
- **"I can bring that person back."** No reading, ritual or remedy makes another adult return or feel differently.
- **Curse and black-magic removal.** A fear-based sale, structured around a problem only the seller can diagnose and only the seller can fix.
- **Urgency plus a large fee.** "This must be done before the next full moon" is a sales technique, not an astrological finding.
- **A percentage accuracy figure.** "99% accurate" is unmeasurable and unverifiable. It appears in this market because it converts, not because anyone has tested it.

## Warning signs in how a consultation is run

Being told something frightening in the first two minutes. Being quoted a remedy before the chart has been read. Being discouraged from getting a second opinion. Being pressed for a decision on the spot. Being told your difficulties trace to a single dosha that only an expensive ritual can address.

## What a good consultation actually feels like

Unremarkable, mostly. You are asked for your birth details and what you want to understand. The chart is explained in language you can follow. Difficult periods are described accurately, without drama. Where nothing needs doing, you are told nothing needs doing — and the consultation ends without a sale.

If you have been quoted a large sum somewhere for a dosha remedy, take the chart for a second reading before you pay anything. It is the cheapest protection available.
