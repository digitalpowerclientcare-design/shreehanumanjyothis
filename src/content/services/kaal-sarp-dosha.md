## What the configuration is

All seven classical planets — Sun, Moon, Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus and Saturn — falling on one side of the axis formed by Rahu and Ketu. There are twelve named types depending on which houses Rahu and Ketu occupy, and each is associated with a different area of emphasis.

A **partial** formation, where one planet sits outside the axis, is common and is generally treated as considerably less significant.

## What it is associated with

Traditionally: results that arrive later than expected, effort meeting more resistance in specific areas, and periods that feel like sustained uphill work. Delay rather than denial.

## What it is not

It is not a curse. It is not an explanation for every difficulty in a life. It does not require an expensive ritual to neutralise. It is one configuration among many in a chart, and it is read alongside everything else — the strength of the lagna lord, the dasha sequence, the placement of Jupiter — not in isolation.

## How this is usually sold, and why we do it differently

Kaal Sarp is among the most commercially exploited findings in Indian astrology, precisely because the name sounds severe. Families are told their difficulties trace to it and quoted substantial sums for remedial rituals.

We assess the configuration, tell you its type and whether it is complete, and explain how much weight it actually carries in your chart. Frequently the honest answer is: less than you have been led to believe.
