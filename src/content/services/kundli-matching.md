## What Guna Milan actually measures

Guna Milan compares eight factors — the Ashtakoot — between two birth charts, each carrying a different weight, totalling 36 points. The eight cover temperament, mental compatibility, physical constitution, family orientation, dominance balance, health indicators, emotional nature and lineage.

| Koota | Points | What it looks at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Varna | 1 | Working temperament |
| Vashya | 2 | Mutual influence and give-and-take |
| Tara | 3 | Health and general fortune |
| Yoni | 4 | Physical and instinctive compatibility |
| Graha Maitri | 5 | Mental and intellectual compatibility |
| Gana | 6 | Broad temperament type |
| Bhakoot | 7 | Emotional bond and family prosperity |
| Nadi | 8 | Health and progeny indicators |

The weighting tells you something on its own: Nadi and Bhakoot together carry 15 of the 36 points, which is why a mismatch in either lowers a score sharply even when the rest of the chart is comfortable.

## Why the score is not the answer

A number out of 36 is a summary, not a conclusion. Two things routinely get missed when a family relies on the score alone:

- **Dasha periods.** Two people may be well matched but entering very different planetary periods. That is a timing question, not a compatibility question, and it does not show in the Guna score.
- **Dosha cancellation.** Several combinations — including many Mangal Dosha cases — are cancelled or substantially reduced by other placements. A software report often flags the dosha and stops there.

We read both charts together, give you the Ashtakoot score, and then explain what the score is hiding in either direction.

## What you receive

A clear verbal explanation during the consultation, covering the Ashtakoot result, any doshas present and whether they are mitigated, the relevant dasha positions for both people, and — where they apply — remedies with the reasoning behind each one. Nothing is recommended that we cannot explain.

## North Indian and South Indian methods

Families from Telugu, Kannada and Marathi backgrounds often use **Porutham** (also called Jataka matching) rather than the North Indian Ashtakoot system. The two examine overlapping ground with different structures and different emphases. If your family follows the Porutham system, see [Jataka Matching](/services/jataka-matching/) — and if the two sides of an alliance follow different traditions, we can read both and explain where they agree and where they diverge.
