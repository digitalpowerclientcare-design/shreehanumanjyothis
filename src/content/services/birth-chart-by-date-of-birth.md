## What your date of birth actually gives us

Three details, and each one unlocks a different layer of the chart:

| Detail | What it determines | If it is missing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Date of birth | Moon sign, Nakshatra, dasha sequence | Nothing can be read at all |
| Time of birth | Lagna and the twelve houses | Career and timing readings lose precision |
| Place of birth | Local planetary positions at that moment | Calculations shift by degrees |

This is why "astrology by date of birth" is a slightly incomplete phrase. The date is the starting point, not the whole input.

## What the chart is read for

- **Career and work** — the tenth house, its lord, and the periods that support a change
- **Marriage and compatibility** — the seventh house, Venus and Jupiter, plus dasha timing
- **Family and property** — the fourth house and its associated periods
- **Health indications** — described as tendencies, never as diagnosis
- **Timing** — which periods are supportive, and which are better spent consolidating

## The honest limits

A chart prepared from your date of birth will not tell you the month you will marry, whether a specific job application succeeds, or how long you will live. It describes patterns and periods.

Where a reading is uncertain — most often because the birth time is approximate — you will be told which conclusions still hold and which do not. That distinction is the whole difference between a reading and a guess.

## Free reports and why they read the same for everyone

Automated date-of-birth reports are widely available and the arithmetic behind them is usually correct. What they cannot do is read placements against each other, weigh a strong Jupiter against an afflicted seventh house, or answer the question you actually came with.

If you have already generated one, bring it. We will go through what it got right and what it flattened.
